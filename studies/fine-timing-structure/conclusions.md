# Completed answers: useful sensitivity, no measured departure

**Finer timing adds demonstrable power for specific digital alternatives, but
this archive does not produce a detected difference or resolved positive J in
the frozen measured family.** It also does not give a strong, general fine-law
TV constraint. The gain is a quantified sensitivity map, not new physics.

## 1. Does any declared test detect a difference?

No. None of the 24 receiver/local-setting/assignment/missingness cases has a
positive K_grid or TV lower bound. These are overlapping assumption sensitivities,
not 24 independent null experiments. Under epsilon=0 and event-supported
completion, simultaneous K_grid upper bounds are 39.3,64.8,42.2,63.1 ppm
(Alice 0/1, Bob 0/1). Monotone bounds covering the retained phases between grid
cuts are 47.9,91.2,54.4,103.8 ppm. The latter are weaker because within-cell
locations are not identified by the committed one-tag-cell histograms.
Under unrestricted missingness at epsilon=0, K_grid upper bounds instead span
225.7–246.1 ppm. At epsilon=.01 with event-supported completion they span
53.9–101.4 ppm. The ideal/event-supported headline is conditional, not a
robustness claim across all assumptions.

## 2. Do the coarse categories conceal a measured difference?

No positive J=TV_fine-TV_coarse lower bound is resolved. The exact first-event
coarse counts, exposure and uncertainty from #13 reproduce from the new fine
extraction. The original #13 coarse TV intervals remain unchanged. A difference
between the original and extension confidence bounds reflects different family
costs and consistency constraints, not different coarse records.

The simulations demonstrate a real capability of the method: moving core mass
to opposite legal cells pk and pk+1 leaves the coarse law exactly unchanged.
At original archive size with event-supported uncertainty, a 30.2 ppm Alice/y0
alternative is detected by width1 in 400/400 repetitions and by the CDF test in
158/400, while coarse detects 0/400. These are generated alternatives, not effects
found in the measured records. Exact 95% pointwise Monte Carlo intervals are
reported for every rate; 400/400 has a lower limit about 99.08%.

## 3. How large a digital redistribution can remain unrecovered?

For the frozen within-core alternatives at archive size:

| Receiver/y | Injected fine TV (ppm) | CDF recoveries /400 | width1 recoveries /400 | Positive J /400 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Alice/0 | 15.1 | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| Alice/1 | 18.5 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Bob/0 | 7.9 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Bob/1 | 25.0 | 0 | 385 | 0 |

The direction and support matter, not just a scalar TV magnitude. Even detecting
a deliberately inserted difference need not certify positive J: Alice/y0's
60.3 ppm alternative is detected by both fine tests in 400/400, yet J resolves
in 0/400 because the coarse upper uncertainty is still too large. At larger
injections, J becomes resolvable: Bob/y1 at 124.9 ppm resolves it in 400/400.
These are model-specific power statements. They neither exclude all such effects
from the real archive nor convert to physical picosecond sensitivity.

## 4. Does extra resolution improve practical sensitivity?

Yes for some alternatives, and at a cost. In the sparse empirical-background
model, the examples above establish power beyond the exact coarse mapping. The
full recovery output compares five resolutions, two completion budgets and
0.25/1/4 times the sample size on paired realizations. Pulse identity and bin
boundaries interact with the alternative; no representation wins uniformly.
An event-rate-only change can be detected without any timing-shape change.

For general measured-law upper bounds, the cost is substantial. The extension's
coarse TV upper bounds are approximately 61.2,92.4,60.6,88.9 ppm. At width8 and
finer, bounds saturate at the event-incidence cap: 202.2,487.4,175.3,474.9 ppm.
The sparse data plus conservative family and completion costs prevent a useful
general restriction on arbitrary fine redistributions relative to their event
mass. K bounds constrain cumulative differences more usefully, but K and TV are
different targets: opposite changes across many cells can have small K and large
TV. A smaller K upper bound is not a tighter TV bound.

## 5. Which limits belong to the analysis, and which to the measurement?

- **Analysis limits:** fixed grid, chosen hierarchy, conservative finite-feature
  union bound, sparse counts, missingness envelopes and no test inside each
  one-tag phase cell. The full raw archive/cache retains phase detail the tested
  grid discards. This study does not prove that no other justified method can do
  better; no subsequent boundary or subset search is included here.
- **Archived measurement limits:** the exact ideal-quantizer witness from #13
  remains: different analog laws can produce identical full digitized laws.
  Digital analysis cannot distinguish those laws without added response-model
  assumptions. This is a model-specific information-loss fact, not a claim that
  the actual apparatus realizes the constructed latent laws.
- **Physical interpretation limits:** assignment exogeneity, trial correspondence,
  recorded detector completeness and physical timing are premises. Nothing here
  certifies them or establishes superluminal influence. First-event results do
  not characterize the entire point process. Both receivers are one run, not
  independent experimental replications.

The fixed empirical family, controls and source checks are complete. The stopping
rule is satisfied: quantify what the retained resolution buys and stop, without
expanding the search in response to these results. Literature novelty remains
unestablished; the primary-method comparisons are explicit in sources.md.
