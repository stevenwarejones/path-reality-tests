# Validation — phase-intervention design

Synthetic checks run successfully with Python 3.12 and NumPy 2.3.5. The script was
invoked from a different working directory to verify that its output paths are
relative to its own folder. No experimental input data were used.

`intervention_checks.py --check` compares recomputed values with the reviewed snapshot without rewriting outputs; running
without the flag regenerates `intervention.json`. Python syntax compilation also
passes. With seed 3212026, the checks cover:

- 48 mixed-state/effect cases comparing the phase formula with direct
  density-matrix evolution, including the imaginary contrast sign. Maximum
  numerical difference is below 2.23×10⁻¹⁶.
- The constant prediction of the specified dephased model.
- A lossy balanced example with selected-bin, other-bin and failure outcomes
  summing to one at every setting.
- A coherent input with zero contrast for a phase-insensitive effect, showing why
  a null result alone does not establish dephasing.
- An intermediate-projection counterexample: unrestricted final-bin probability
  1 versus projected probability 1/4, survival 1/2 and conditioned probability 1/2.
- 32 random checks of the telescoping norm-error bound.
- The declared precision calculation: with family error 0.01 and 10,000 trials
  per phase, the simultaneous contrast radius is approximately 0.036564; a target
  radius of 0.02 requires 33,424 trials per phase under the stated assumptions.

The numbers are generated by the script, not copied from experimental data.
Numerical examples do not prove the general identities. The separate formal companion derives the finite-dimensional quantum phase
identity, dephased invariance and deterministic calibrated-null bound. The
Hoeffding coverage/power calculation and continuum/window correspondence
remain analytical arguments, not Lean-certified statistical or apparatus claims. The fixed-sample precision calculation assumes
independent Bernoulli trials with fixed setting probabilities; it is not an
empirical power calculation or validation of calibration, drift or trial selection.

No apparatus has been calibrated and no scientific hypothesis has been rejected
by these checks. Physical feasibility and calibration remain open in PH01–PH05 and PH07–PH08.
PH06 is closed at the literature scope: this is a re-analysis of known interference.

`design.py --check` verifies the prospective sample-size and occupation-power
snapshot without rewriting outputs. The binomial tests directly sum probability
masses to check CP coverage over 101 probabilities at n=1,7,40, endpoint
monotonicity, unequal-count simultaneous coverage and null rejection risk.
Small-design exact power independently exceeds the claimed certificate.
Five-context power checks verify both binomial-envelope tails and every corner
of the count envelope, including worst-case occupation. Strict equality is
inconclusive; integer sample plans are checked at their returned sizes.
The question-register test checks every Q-ID against the completion report,
including grouped ranges. Existing tests continue to cover integrity failures,
replication, synthetic algebra, corruption, and invocation from other directories.

Each numerical JSON records its generating script and SHA-256. CI checks
snapshots without overwriting them. The guarantees remain conditional on the
fixed-probability independent trial model derived in the protocol; these tests
supply no calibration or observed experimental power.
